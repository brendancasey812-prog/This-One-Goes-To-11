Who am I?

A researcher who finally has time to go places.

I'm a semi-retired Alzheimer's and brain health researcher living in the San Francisco Bay Area with my husband, Brian, and our semi-famous Cat from Hell, Autumn. We have two grown kids who sometimes still like to travel with us.

I'm at a place in my life where I'm ready to explore the world, and I do a LOT of research before going on trips. I want to share what I learn with you, to make it easier for you to plan amazing trips.

I'm not in this to make money — just to share knowledge. Nothing here is sponsored, and I haven't been compensated for any of the activities I write about.

What will you learn from me?

Outside all day. Somewhere excellent for dinner.

My favorite travel experiences include a combination of natural beauty, urban culture, and great food. My favorite things are usually outdoors — hiking, biking, lounging — but I also enjoy a good museum. Either way, I want to reward myself with a fabulous meal.

So if that's what you like, keep reading. Every trip here comes with the research attached: when to go, what it costs, what's worth the queue, and what politely isn't.

Why "This One Goes to 11"?

One louder.

For people of a certain age, the 1984 mockumentary This Is Spinal Tap by Rob Reiner is part of our zeitgeist. In one of the most iconic scenes, guitarist Nigel Tufnel is showing off his custom guitar amplifier, pointing out that its volume knobs are "one louder" than standard amps because they go up to 11 instead of 10.

When asked by the interviewer why he doesn't simply make the volume of 10 louder, Tufnel replies, "But this one goes to 11."

For me, taking something to 11 means doing everything in my power to make an experience as amazing as possible. That's the whole editorial policy, really: if a trip only ever got to ten, it doesn't end up here.

How to read these guides

  • Why this trip goes to 11 — what made it worth the effort.
  • The planning bits — timing, weather, driving, what to book early.
  • Top 11 — the things I'd actually tell you to do, in order.
  • Logistics — who we booked with and what it cost.

Prices are what we paid, in local currency with a rough USD conversion. Treat them as a ballpark, not a quote.
